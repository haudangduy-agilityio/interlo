# Code of Conduct

This code of conduct is adapted from the Contributor Covenant, version 2.1,
which is licensed under CC BY 4.0. The original text is at
https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/

## Our pledge

We pledge to make taking part in Interlo a harassment-free experience for
everyone, whatever their age, body size, visible or invisible disability,
ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of
experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance,
race, caste, colour, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We act and interact in ways that help build an open, welcoming, diverse,
inclusive and healthy community.

## Our standards

Behaviour that helps the community:

- Showing empathy and kindness to other people.
- Respecting different opinions, viewpoints and experiences.
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback.
- Taking responsibility for our mistakes, apologising to those affected, and
  learning from them.
- Focusing on what is best for the community as a whole, not only for us.

Behaviour that is not acceptable:

- Sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing other people's private information, such as a home address or an
  email address, without their explicit permission.
- Other conduct that could reasonably be seen as inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

## Enforcement responsibilities

Project maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these
standards. They will take fair and appropriate action in response to behaviour
they find inappropriate, threatening, offensive or harmful. They may remove,
edit or reject comments, commits, code, issues and other contributions that do
not follow this code of conduct, and will give the reasons when they do.

## Scope

This code of conduct applies in all project spaces: the repository, its issues,
pull requests and discussions. It also applies when a person officially
represents the project in a public space.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report conduct problems privately. Use the private reporting channel described
in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) (a confidential issue on GitLab, or private vulnerability reporting on GitHub). Do not open a public issue or pull request about
a conduct problem. All reports are reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly.
Maintainers keep the reporter's identity private.

Maintainers follow these steps, in this order, when they decide on a
consequence:

1. **Correction.** A private, written warning with the reason and a clear
   statement of what was inappropriate. A public apology may be asked for.
2. **Warning.** A warning with consequences for continued behaviour. No contact
   with the people involved, including the people who enforce this code, for a
   set period.
3. **Temporary ban.** A temporary ban from any public interaction with the
   project for a set period.
4. **Permanent ban.** A permanent ban from any public interaction with the
   project, for a pattern of violations or for harassment of a person.
